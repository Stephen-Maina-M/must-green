About the Tree Species Guide

Trees are an important part of healthy ecosystems. They provide habitats for living organisms, protect soil, improve landscapes and contribute to environmental sustainability.

This guide introduces three important tree groups that can be considered in environmental conservation and tree-planting activities: Pine, East African Cedar and Mahogany.

1. Pine

Scientific Name: Pinus spp.

Description

Pine refers to a group of evergreen coniferous trees belonging to the genus Pinus. Pine trees are generally recognised by their needle-like leaves, woody cones and relatively tall, straight growth. Different pine species vary in size and environmental requirements.

Key Characteristics

  • They are generally evergreen trees.
  • They have needle-like leaves.
  • They produce woody cones containing seeds.
  • Many species develop tall and relatively straight trunks.

Environmental Benefits

Pine trees can contribute to vegetation cover, provide habitats for organisms, protect soil and improve the appearance of green spaces. Their contribution depends on the species and the environment where they are planted.

Uses

  • Timber production
  • Wood products
  • Landscaping
  • Shade and recreational spaces

Planting and Care

Pine species should be planted in locations suitable for the particular species. Young trees should be protected from damage, competing vegetation and fire, and should receive appropriate care during establishment.

2. East African Cedar

Scientific Name: Juniperus procera

Description

East African Cedar is an evergreen conifer native to the highlands of eastern Africa. It can develop a tall trunk and a spreading crown. It is valued for its timber, landscape value and contribution to highland ecosystems.

Key Characteristics

  • It is an evergreen coniferous tree.
  • It can grow into a large tree under suitable conditions.
  • It has small scale-like or needle-like foliage.
  • It can develop a strong and durable trunk.

Environmental Benefits

East African Cedar contributes to vegetation cover and can provide habitat for organisms. Trees in highland ecosystems can also contribute to soil protection and the maintenance of green landscapes.

Uses

  • Timber
  • Construction materials
  • Furniture and wood products
  • Landscaping

Planting and Care

Cedar should be planted in a suitable environment with enough space for the tree to develop. Young trees should be protected from physical damage, fire and competing vegetation.

3. Mahogany

Scientific Name: Khaya anthotheca

Description

Mahogany is a valuable hardwood tree associated with tropical and subtropical regions of Africa. It can develop a large crown and straight trunk. Mahogany is highly valued for its quality timber and is also important in forestry and environmental conservation.

Key Characteristics

  • It is a large hardwood tree.
  • It develops a broad crown when given enough space.
  • It has compound leaves.
  • It can develop a strong and valuable timber trunk.

Environmental Benefits

Mahogany trees contribute to forest cover and can provide habitats for various organisms. Large trees also contribute to the structure and ecological functions of forest ecosystems.

Uses

  • High-quality timber
  • Furniture
  • Cabinet making
  • Construction and wood products
  • Landscaping and shade

Planting and Care

Mahogany requires sufficient space for healthy growth. Young trees should be protected and properly maintained while they become established.

Why Trees Matter

Trees are essential components of healthy ecosystems. They contribute to biodiversity, protect soil, provide habitats, improve landscapes and support human activities through products such as timber and fruit.

Protecting and planting appropriate tree species can therefore contribute to the creation of greener and more sustainable university surroundings.

Choosing the Right Tree

  1. Consider the climate and environmental conditions.
  2. Consider the type and condition of the soil.
  3. Ensure there is enough space for the mature tree.
  4. Select species that are suitable for the intended purpose.
  5. Plan for proper care and maintenance after planting.
